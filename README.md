# what-got-me-into-bmx My Freestyle BMX Journey

People often ask me what inspired me to start riding freestyle BMX.

To answer that, I have to go back to when I was a kid.

I grew up riding bikes with my friends almost every day. We’d spend hours outside building small dirt jumps, riding through neighborhoods, and seeing who could go the fastest or jump the highest. At the time, it was simply something we loved doing. None of us realized those moments would shape who we would become years later.

As I got older, I started discovering freestyle BMX through videos, magazines, and professional riders. Watching athletes perform tricks that seemed impossible completely changed the way I looked at riding. I wasn’t just seeing people on bicycles anymore—I was watching individuals express creativity, precision, athleticism, and years of dedication.

That curiosity quickly became an obsession.

I wanted to understand how those tricks were possible. I wanted to know how riders could control a bike so precisely while flying through the air, balancing on one wheel, or spinning in multiple directions. Instead of simply watching, I wanted to experience that feeling for myself.

When I first began learning freestyle BMX, I realized very quickly that it wasn’t easy.

I crashed more times than I can remember. I dealt with frustration, fear, and self-doubt. Some days I made progress, while other days it felt like I was starting over from the beginning.

Looking back, I’m grateful for those challenges because they taught me lessons that extend far beyond riding a bike.

Freestyle BMX is one of the greatest teachers of patience I’ve ever experienced.

Every trick is a process of learning. You analyze what went wrong, make small adjustments, build confidence, and try again. Improvement rarely happens overnight. Instead, it comes from hundreds—sometimes thousands—of small repetitions that gradually build into mastery.

That mindset has changed the way I approach every challenge in life.

BMX has taught me that failure isn’t something to avoid—it’s valuable feedback. Every unsuccessful attempt provides information that helps you improve on the next one. Instead of viewing mistakes as setbacks, I’ve learned to see them as opportunities to grow.

Another reason freestyle BMX means so much to me is because it combines athletic performance with creativity.

No two riders have exactly the same style. Every rider approaches tricks differently, chooses different lines, and expresses themselves in a unique way. That individuality is one of the things that makes freestyle BMX such an incredible sport.

As I’ve continued riding over the years, I’ve also become fascinated by the physical side of performance.

I realized that becoming a better rider isn’t only about practicing tricks. Strength, mobility, endurance, balance, recovery, and overall fitness all contribute to how well you perform on the bike.

That’s what eventually led me to combine strength training with freestyle BMX.

The stronger, healthier, and more conditioned I became, the more confidence I developed on my bike. I wasn’t just improving physically—I was creating a foundation that allowed me to ride longer, recover faster, and continue progressing.

Today, freestyle BMX is much more than a hobby to me.

It’s a constant reminder that meaningful progress takes time, discipline, and persistence. Every new trick represents another opportunity to learn, adapt, and become a better version of myself.

If there’s one message I hope people take away from my story, it’s this:

Don’t be discouraged by being a beginner.

Every skilled rider you admire once struggled with the basics. Every difficult trick was once impossible for them. The difference is that they stayed committed to the process instead of giving up when things became difficult.

Success in BMX—and in life—is rarely about natural talent alone.

More often, it’s the result of consistency, curiosity, and the willingness to keep showing up even when progress feels slow.

That’s what freestyle BMX has taught me, and that’s why I’ll continue riding for as long as I’m able.
